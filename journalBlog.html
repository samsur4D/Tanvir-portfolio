Biosynthesis of ZnO-TiO2 nanocomposites by using Azadirachta indica and Ocimum Tenuiflorum extract; A comparative study of their microstructural, antibacterial, and photocatalytic properties

Abstract

In this study, ZnO-TiO2 nanocomposites were produced using a low-cost and environmentally friendly Biosynthesis method devoid of any toxic chemicals. Herein, the structural, antibacterial, photocatalytic, and optical properties of ZT NCs along with Neem (Azadirachta indica) (ZTN) and Tulsi (Ocimum Tenuiflorum) (ZTT) extracts, were investigated. All the NCs formed a hexagonal wurtzite phase, anatase, and hexagonal ZnTiO3, as confirmed by XRD analysis. The crystallite sizes of the generated NCs ranged from 16.92 to 22.52 nm, with ZTN being the lowest of the lot. The morphological analysis through SEM confirmed that most of the particles of ZT remain between 25-60 nm, whereas for ZTN NCs, the range lies from 15-45 nm and 20-50 nm for ZTT NCs. Additionally, the EDX analysis corroborated the presence of ZN, Ti, and O without any impurity. The antibacterial activity analysis was performed using the agar well diffusion method, involving both a Gram-positive bacterium (Staphylococcus aureus) and a Gram-negative bacterium (Escherichia coli) as the bacterium medium. In both variants, the zone of inhibition (ZOI) of bacterial expansion obtained was much higher for Neem extract (18.2 mm and 22.5mm, respectively), mainly due to its higher surface area and reduced particle size. The absorption bands of the ZT, ZTN, and ZTT NCs variants were 378 nm, 370 nm, and 300 nm, respectively. Similarly, ZTN NCs (3.48 eV) exhibited a wider optical band gap energy than those it’s ZTT NCs (3.40 eV) and ZT NCs (3.37 eV) counterparts. The addition of the green extracts resulted in an increment of the band gap and transparency of the NCs while reducing their absorption coefficient. The light-driven catalytic capabilities of the nanocomposites were assessed by the breakdown of methylene blue dye.
